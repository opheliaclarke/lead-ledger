How it works

The whole model, in one table.

The thing that makes this look complicated is that who paid for the lead and who collects the money come apart. As long as both ends belong to one person, nothing moves. A debt only appears when they split — and that happens in exactly three of the six lanes.

NET = what Olivia owes Bob − what Bob owes Olivia ± the other cost, if you settle it

Positive means Olivia pays Bob. Negative means Bob pays Olivia. Daily nets simply add up, so settling every day, every week or once a month gives the identical figure.

Each day

  1. Add a day and put the date on it.
  2. Put the lead counts in — how many came from Bob's campaign, from S, from GS, and whether each went to Olivia or to Bob's private buyer.
  3. Prices come from the Rates tab automatically. Switch on per-day prices only if a price changed that day.
  4. If there was another cost, put the amount, who paid it, and whether it settles between them.
  5. Read the last column. It says who pays whom, and how much.
The full write-up
What this can't catch. A lead count typed wrong, a lead logged in the wrong lane, the wrong person named as the payer, or a price typed wrong — all of those calculate perfectly and still send the wrong amount. The arithmetic is safe; the typing is not. Check the counts against the panel's own report before anyone transfers money.
Everything calculates in the page as you type — no spreadsheet, no server, nothing to install. The arithmetic runs in whole hundredths so nothing drifts, and it matches the Excel model cell for cell. How it was checked.
