Lead ledger · Bob & Olivia

Who owes whom, worked out every day

Three lead sources, two sets of buyers, one panel. This is the sheet that turns the day's lead counts into a single number: how much moves between Bob and Olivia.

1The whole setup, in six lanes

Every lead that exists is one of these six. Three of them create a debt between Bob and Olivia. The other three don't — both ends of the deal already belong to the same person.

The thing that makes this look complicated is that who paid for the lead and who collects the money come apart. As long as both ends belong to one person, nothing moves. A debt only appears when they split — and that happens in exactly three of the six lanes.

2The one rule

NET  =  what Olivia owes Bob  −  what Bob owes Olivia  ±  the other cost, if you settle it

The NET is always written from Olivia's side. Positive means Olivia pays Bob. Negative means Bob pays Olivia. It is the last column of the Daily tab, and it is the only number you have to read.

Daily nets simply add up. Settling every day, every week or once a month gives the identical figure, so you can leave it running and settle whenever you like.

3What you type, and what the sheet does

  1. Put the date in the first column.
  2. Put the six lead counts in — how many came from Bob's campaign, from S, from GS, and whether each went to Olivia or to Bob's private buyer. Leave a lane blank if it didn't happen.
  3. The three price columns fill themselves from the Rates tab. Type over one only if the price changed that day — the price is never buried inside a formula, so a price change can't quietly rewrite last month's numbers.
  4. If there was any other cost, put the amount, who paid it, and YES or NO for whether it settles between you.
  5. Read the last column. It says, in words, who pays whom and how much.

5What stops it going wrong

The Summary tab carries four checks. Each one rebuilds a figure by a different route than the column it is checking, so a broken formula shows up as a number instead of hiding.

CheckHow it is worked out differently
1. Lead moneyMultiplies the lead counts by the prices itself, instead of trusting the two money columns.
2. Other costRe-reads the amount, the payer and the YES/NO, instead of trusting the adjustment column.
3. The NET columnCatches a NET cell that has had a number typed straight into it.
4. Missing priceCounts days priced at nothing. Should never be anything but zero.

I broke the file six different ways on purpose and every one was caught, with the check reading back the exact size of the error.

What they cannot see, and I'd rather say it than let you find out later: a lead count typed wrong, leads logged in the wrong lane, the wrong person named as the payer, or a price typed wrong on the Rates tab. All four pass every check silently and still move the money — in my test, by as much as 64,368. Zero means the formulas are intact. It does not mean the numbers are right. Only the panel's own report does that.

6Four things I had to assume — tell me if any is wrong

1. What does Bob pay Olivia for a GS lead that goes to his private buyer?

This is the one real gap. Olivia paid GS for that lead and Bob collected the money for it, so something has to come back — but you've never said how much.

I've set it to whatever GS costs Olivia, so it passes straight through at cost with no margin either way. One cell on the Rates tab. If you agreed a different number, or Olivia takes a margin on it, type that instead and everything downstream follows.

2. Does Bob pay anything for using Olivia's panel?

His private-buyer leads are delivered through her panel. If there's a per-lead fee for that, it belongs in the settlement.

I've set the fee to 0, so today it does nothing. If there is one, put the number in and it starts charging on Bob's private-buyer leads automatically.

3. An "other cost" marked YES — does the other person pay it all back, or half?

You said add a column for other costs, who paid, and whether it counts in the final number. What you didn't say is how much of it counts.

I've built it as paid back in full: if Bob paid it and it's marked YES, Olivia reimburses the whole amount. If it should be split down the middle instead, say so and it's a one-line change.

4. Is GLU the only private buyer, and is Olivia's price the same from every source?

You said "private buyers" plural but only named GLU. And you said Bob's price to Olivia is always the same — I've taken that to mean the same whether the lead came from his campaign or from S.

All of Bob's private buyers are counted together in one bucket, because for this question it doesn't matter which of them bought — it only matters that the buyer was Bob's and not Olivia's. If you want to see GLU separately from the others, that's a wider sheet and I'd need to know how many there are.

7One thing worth watching

The Summary tab has a block for money already transferred. Log every payment there. A ledger like this keeps totalling up what is owed in principle; unless the cash that has actually moved is written down next to it, the balance keeps asking for money that has already been paid. The "still outstanding" figure at the bottom is the one to act on.
